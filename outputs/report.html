Online Advertising Performance

Company X / Advert Firm A • 1 April–30 June 2020 • USD

Revenue belongs to Advert Firm A; it is Company X’s recorded ad spend. Firm CPC = cost/clicks; Company X CPC = revenue/clicks. Firm ROI = (revenue−cost)/cost. Company X ROAS = post-click sales/revenue. Company X profit ROI cannot be calculated without product margins and other costs. All rates use sums of numerators divided by sums of denominators.

Engagement is a pre-existing audience category, not an observed continuous score. CTR is used as a behavioral proxy. Conversion rate here means attributed transactions per click, not the probability that a unique person converts. The file has transactions greater than clicks in some rows and no unique-user IDs. Attribution is over 30 days: the extraction date and maturity of June outcomes are unknown. Results describe associations, not causal effects.

Data checks: 15,408 rows, 91 dates, 3 campaigns, 8 banners; 413 missing placements retained as Unknown. Two empty columns removed. 5 exact duplicate rows retained because no unique record ID establishes whether they are errors; duplicate_sensitivity.csv shows their impact. 329 rows have transactions above clicks and 31 have transactions without clicks; 2 have clicks above displays. Machine learning: conditional click prediction

Task: predict clicks for a campaign/banner/placement/audience/date, given an impression volume. Actual displays are available in this offline evaluation; for a future scenario you must supply a planned display count. This is not an unconditional forecast or a user-level conversion classifier. Features: audience and campaign categories, banner, placement, log(1+displays), banner area, weekday, and days since 1 April. Target: clicks. Excluded: cost, revenue, conversions, and sales, because they would leak outcomes or be unavailable beforehand.

April trains the candidates, May chooses the smallest MAE, and June is held out. The candidates are an exposure-based baseline (training CTR × displays) and two Poisson gradient-boosting models. Selected on May: Poisson boosting (15 leaves). The selected method is refit on April+May before June evaluation. Lower MAE and RMSE are better; R² may be negative under distribution shift. June is not used for model choice. No claim of production readiness is made; later-period data and rolling validation are needed.
